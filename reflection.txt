Recursive insertion works by using the same method over and over, but on levels that are progressively deeper 
into the tree. So the function is always working on just one level, but if the requisite identifiers are not 
presently found, the method calls on another instance of itself to reference the next level down in the tree. 
The main challenge that I faced when figuring out where to put a new employee was figuring out how to utilize 
recursion properly. So, to summarize, my main problem in this assignment was figuring out how to properly and 
most efficiently utilize recursion within the same function in order to achieve my goal of iterating through the 
established binary tree of a company's organizational structure in order to insert new employee entries in their 
specified positions within the established hierarchy. I definitely had a lot of help from the Rize resources. 
Trees might be preferable to other data structures in real-world systems when those aforementioned systems are 
more of a hierarchy instead of simply keeping track of names in a one-dimensional order, like I have used 
previously in this course. This is particularly the case when the hierarchy begins to expand beyond just a 
couple of levels, and even more so when those levels gradually accumulate more and more entries.
